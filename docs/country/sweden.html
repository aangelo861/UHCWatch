How many households face financial hardship?

In 2015, 1.6% of households had catastrophic health spending. In the poorest fifth of households it was 5.8%; in the richest fifth, 0.6%. Among households with catastrophic spending, dental care accounted for the largest share of out-of-pocket payments (56.8%). 1.0% of households were impoverished or further impoverished by out-of-pocket payments (2015).

Who goes without care?

In 2025, 2.1% of people had unmet need for health care: 3.0% in the poorest fifth and 1.6% in the richest. In 2025, 2.6% of people had unmet need for dental care: 4.9% in the poorest fifth and 0.5% in the richest. 8.2% of people needing care could not afford a prescribed medicine (2019).

How much do people pay out of pocket?

Out-of-pocket payments were 13.4% of current spending on health in 2024. The median across 53 countries is 22.0%. Households spent 2.1% of their budget on out-of-pocket payments for health care (2015).

How much does government spend on health?

Public spending on health was 9.6% of GDP in 2024. The median across 53 countries is 5.5%. Health took 19.4% of government spending (2023).

Where are the gaps in coverage?

Main gaps in population coverage

Entitlement to publicly financed health care is based on residence, which means all residents are automatically covered. Adults without a resident permit (e.g. asylum seekers and undocumented migrants), refugees and people under the EU Temporary Protection Directive 2001/55/EC are not entitled to the full range of publicly financed health care and can only access emergency care, with co-payments. Administrative barriers may delay access to health care for migrants due to the lengthy residence permit application process.

Main gaps in service coverage

The publicly financed benefits package is relatively comprehensive. However, waiting times are an issue for primary care and outpatient specialist care. They are the main reason for self-reported unmet need for health care, although levels of unmet need for health care are below the European Union average.

Main gaps caused by user charges

Co-payments are applied to most publicly financed health care except diagnostic tests and inpatient medicines. For covered outpatient prescribed medicines, medical products and dental care, patients pay the full price up to a specific threshold. Balance billing is permitted for dental care. No groups of people are exempt from co-payments on the basis of income. Although people with low incomes can apply for retrospective reimbursement of all co-payments, the application process may be an administrative barrier. There is no overall cap on co-payments.
