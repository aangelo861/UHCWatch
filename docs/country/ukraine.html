Ukraine page

How many households face financial hardship?

In 2021, 17.1% of households had catastrophic health spending. In the poorest fifth of households it was 64.2%; in the richest fifth, 2.3%. Among households with catastrophic spending, inpatient care accounted for the largest share of out-of-pocket payments (44.2%). 11.0% of households were impoverished or further impoverished by out-of-pocket payments (2021).

How much do people pay out of pocket?

Out-of-pocket payments were 45.3% of current spending on health in 2021. The median across 53 countries is 22.0%. Households spent 5.4% of their budget on out-of-pocket payments for health care (2021).

How much does government spend on health?

Public spending on health was 4.3% of GDP in 2021. The median across 53 countries is 5.5%. Health took 10.6% of government spending (2021).

Where are the gaps in coverage?

Main gaps in population coverage

People who do not have any formal identification may face barriers to registering with a family doctor, which is a requirement for access to publicly financed health care. While refugees are entitled to the same health services as citizens, asylum seekers and undocumented migrants are entitled to emergency care only and must pay the full cost after treatment.

Main gaps in service coverage

Coverage of medicines, medical products and dental care is limited. The list of covered outpatient medicines (Affordable Medicines Program) is expanding but does not cover many essential medicines. The list of covered inpatient medicines (the Ukrainian Essential Medicines List) is also small and access is further limited by budget constraints and inefficiencies in procuring and distributing medicines. Adults only have access to emergency dental care. Informal payments are widespread, particularly in hospitals.

Main gaps caused by user charges

User charges are limited to outpatient prescribed medicines. The range of covered medicines is small, however, and people frequently pay out of pocket, even for covered services, due to underfunding and other inefficiencies.

VHI role in filling gaps in coverage

VHI mainly plays a supplementary role, providing faster access to treatment and greater choice of provider. It covers around 3% of the population and accounted for 2% of current spending on health in 2021. Expanding VHI is likely to increase inequality in access to health care.

Source: UHC watch policy explorer.

What does UHC Watch recommend?

UHC Watch’s recommendations for improving financial protection in Ukraine, in its own words.

Prioritize public spending on comprehensive primary care, which includes funding for better access to medicines and diagnostic tests, and strengthen the prescribing, dispensing, price regulation and availability of medicines

Continue to expand the Affordable Medicines Program (AMP), so that it better meets the health needs of the population, and ensure that the medicines it covers are cost-effective, aligned with clinical guidelines and target the needs of households with low incomes.

Develop incentives and other mechanisms to stimulate INN prescribing by doctors and the dispensing of cheaper medicines by pharmacies, and monitor prescribing and dispensing under the AMP. Ensure an equitable distribution of pharmacies participating in the AMP across oblasts.

Expand the UEML and monitor spending on inpatient medicines. This should be accompanied by rules to prevent providers from asking people to pay for medicines covered by the UEML and by the monitoring of providers.

Reduce informal payments for inpatient care

Curb ‘charitable donations’ through better enforcement of the provider-purchaser contract provisions that already prohibit these charges and by monitoring providers. Ensure that health care providers do not induce the use of services on the negative list and that there is no double billing of covered services.

Develop a comprehensive and long-term strategy to tackle the root causes of informal payments and implement strategies to improve people’s understanding of their health care entitlements.

Improve the process for reviewing the Program of Medical Guarantees

The process for defining the publicly financed benefits package should be more explicit, transparent and inclusive of a range of perspectives.

Strengthen the capacity of the National Health Service of Ukraine (NHSU) to be an active purchaser of services, which will allow it to make better use of public resources

Enhance protection for households with low incomes and others at high risk of catastrophic health spending

The Government will need to continue to give priority to health in allocating public spending. It should also support the NHSU so that it is better able to reduce out-of-pocket payments for the services that cause financial hardship and to account for changing health needs in the context of the war and growing poverty.

Source: ‘Can people afford to pay for health care? New evidence on financial protection in Ukraine 2023’ (2023) and WHO Country Office in Ukraine (2023).
