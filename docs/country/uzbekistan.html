UHC Watch summary

At present, we do not have numbers on financial hardship for Uzbekistan. See Policy explorer for information on coverage policy.

Policy explorer

How much do people pay out of pocket?

Out-of-pocket payments were 64.1% of current spending on health in 2023. The median across 53 countries is 22.0%.

How much does government spend on health?

Public spending on health was 2.4% of GDP in 2023. The median across 53 countries is 5.5%. Health took 7.9% of government spending (2023).

Where are the gaps in coverage?

Main gaps in population coverage

Refugees, asylum seekers and undocumented migrants are only entitled to publicly financed emergency care, care in childbirth and care for complications during pregnancy.

Main gaps in service coverage

Coverage of outpatient medicines, medical products, dental care, rehabilitation and palliative care is very limited.

Informal payments to health workers are an issue in inpatient care.

Availability of medicines is an issue in inpatient care.

A shortage of family doctors disproportionately affects rural areas.

Substantial regional differences in the level of public spending on health per person suggest that access to health care varies across the country."

Main gaps caused by user charges

Although there are no user charges for covered health care, underfunding of the State Guaranteed Benefits Package results in informal payments, particularly in inpatient facilities. Extra billing for non-covered services also leads to out-of-pocket payments.

VHI role in filling gaps in coverage

VHI plays a complementary (services) role. It accounted for 0.1% of current spending on health in 2021. Expanding VHI is likely to increase inequality in access to health care.
