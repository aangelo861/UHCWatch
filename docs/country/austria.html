How many households face financial hardship?

In 2020, 4.2% of households had catastrophic health spending. In the poorest fifth of households it was 14.9%; in the richest fifth, 1.1%. Among households with catastrophic spending, dental care accounted for the largest share of out-of-pocket payments (29.6%). 1.9% of households were impoverished or further impoverished by out-of-pocket payments (2020).

Who goes without care?

In 2024, 1.0% of people had unmet need for health care: 1.6% in the poorest fifth and 0.6% in the richest. In 2024, 1.1% of people had unmet need for dental care: 3.1% in the poorest fifth and 0.4% in the richest. 3.2% of people needing care could not afford a prescribed medicine (2019).

How much do people pay out of pocket?

Out-of-pocket payments were 16.5% of current spending on health in 2023. The median across 53 countries is 22.0%. Households spent 4.7% of their budget on out-of-pocket payments for health care (2020).

How much does government spend on health?

Public spending on health was 9.0% of GDP in 2024. The median across 53 countries is 5.5%. Health took 16.2% of government spending (2023).

Where are the gaps in coverage?

Main gaps in population coverage

A small share of the population (0.01–0.02% depending on the data source) lacks social health insurance (SHI) scheme coverage. Undocumented migrants are not entitled to SHI coverage. Because entitlement to SHI benefits is based on payment of contributions, some people without stable employment, people with serious mental health problems, homeless people and some unemployed people (women in particular) also lack coverage. More recent administrative data suggest that people lacking SHI coverage include unregistered asylum seekers and students too old to be covered as dependants.

Main gaps in service coverage

Waiting times for elective treatment in public hospitals and related issues with informal payments and incentives to prioritise people with VHI. Some variation in benefits across social health insurance funds. Increasing shortages of medicines, particularly for antimicrobial substances. A lack of contracted psychotherapists and midwives.

Main gaps caused by user charges

Co-payment policy varies across social health insurance schemes and funds for outpatient visits, dental care and medical products and across regions for inpatient care. People with low incomes and people with chronic conditions are not systematically exempt from co-payments. There is variation in the cap on co-payments for medical products and no overall cap on co-payments.

VHI role in filling gaps in coverage

VHI mainly offers access to private physicians, enhanced amenities in hospital and faster access to treatment. It also plays a small complementary role, providing access to some services that are not covered by the social health insurance scheme. For a very small share of the population, VHI plays a substitutive role for people who opt out of the SHI scheme. In 2022 it accounted for 4.6% of current spending on health. Expanding VHI is likely to increase inequality in access to health care.
