How many households face financial hardship?

In 2020, 3.9% of households had catastrophic health spending. In the poorest fifth of households it was 15.8%; in the richest fifth, 0.0%. 2.5% of households were impoverished or further impoverished by out-of-pocket payments (2020).

Who goes without care?

In 2024, 1.0% of people had unmet need for health care: 1.1% in the poorest fifth and 0.9% in the richest. In 2024, 1.3% of people had unmet need for dental care: 3.6% in the poorest fifth and 0.3% in the richest. 3.8% of people needing care could not afford a prescribed medicine (2019).

How much does government spend on health?

Public spending on health was 5.0% of GDP in 2024. The median across 53 countries is 5.5%. Health took 10.9% of government spending (2024).

Where are the gaps in coverage?

Main gaps in population coverage

The main basis for entitlement is payment of contributions to the National Health Fund, which creates a gap in coverage for those who are unable to pay. People in vulnerable situations who cannot pay contributions and are not eligible for social welfare support can obtain coverage by applying to the universal health care coverage pilot set up in 2022, but the number of beneficiaries has been low in relation to the number of people likely to be in need (only 300 as of February 2025).

Refugees are entitled to the same publicly financed benefits as other residents. Asylum seekers are entitled to limited publicly financed health care during a probationary period of three months, after which they are entitled to the same publicly financed benefits as residents.

Undocumented migrant adults are not entitled to any type of publicly financed health care. The children of undocumented migrants are not automatically covered[ST4.1].

Around 7% of the resident population was not covered by the National Health Fund in 2023. Non-covered people include those who do not pay contributions (undocumented migrants), those who do not benefit from the universal health care coverage pilot set up in 2022, EU civil servants (who are covered by a separate EU scheme) and workers who have been in Luxembourg for less than a year and are covered by a health insurance scheme in another country.

Main gaps in service coverage

There are volume limits on doctor consultations and on psychotherapy and physiotherapy sessions. Coverage of optical products (glasses or contact lenses) and some types of dental care is also limited. Retrospective reimbursement applies to some outpatient visits (consultations), which means that people have to pay out of pocket first and then apply for reimbursement from the National Health Fund if their doctor or dentist does not use the ‘direct Immediate payment’ system. This may be a barrier to access for households with low incomes.

Main gaps caused by user charges

There are user charges for all types of care, mainly in the form of percentage co-payments. Balance billing is applied to outpatient primary care visits, outpatient specialist visits, inpatient care and dental care. Dental care treatment is also subject to extra billing. Although there is a cap on most co-payments, and the cap is linked to income, it is not applied automatically and it does not cover balance or extra billing.

VHI role in filling gaps in coverage

VHI accounted for 3.3% of current spending on health in 2024 and covered 67.3% of the population in 2022 (the latest available year of data). VHI does not play a role in filling gaps in population coverage. It covers gaps in service coverage to a limited extent, as it plays a supplementary role covering services already included in the publicly financed benefits package and offering access to enhanced benefits or amenities. VHI covers co-payments for some types of care (inpatient care, dental care and optical care). Coverage varies according to the insurer and insurance plan.
