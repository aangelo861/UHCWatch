UHC Watch summary

At present, we do not have numbers on financial hardship for Azerbaijan. See Policy explorer for information on coverage policy.

Policy explorer

How much do people pay out of pocket?

Out-of-pocket payments were 64.6% of current spending on health in 2023. The median across 53 countries is 22.0%.

How much does government spend on health?

Public spending on health was 1.4% of GDP in 2023. The median across 53 countries is 5.5%. Health took 4.3% of government spending (2023).

Where are the gaps in coverage?

Main gaps in population coverage

There is little information about gaps in coverage. Migrants and refugees are formally entitled to all publicly financed health care but may face administrative barriers to accessing services. Undocumented migrants and asylum seekers are entitled to emergency care only.

Main gaps in service coverage

Coverage of outpatient medicines is extremely limited: medicines are only publicly financed through state vertical programmes covering a handful of conditions (oncology, thalassemia, preventive services for children, vaccination, haemophilia, diabetes, tuberculosis, renal failure and HIV/AIDS). Dental care is not covered. People face barriers to accessing care in rural areas due to poor health infrastructure and workforce shortages. Informal payments are widespread.

Main gaps caused by user charges

User charges are currently applied to diagnostic tests and to specialist care without referral. There are no exemptions from co-payments and there is no cap on co-payments. New user charges are to be introduced in 2025.

VHI role in filling gaps in coverage

VHI plays a supplementary role, providing access to private health care facilities, and a complementary (services) role, granting access to outpatient medicines and dental care. It accounted for 2.4% of current spending on health in 2021. Expanding VHI is likely to increase inequality in access to health care.
