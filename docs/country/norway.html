How many households face financial hardship?

In 2022, 3.1% of households had catastrophic health spending. In the poorest fifth of households it was 6.5%; in the richest fifth, 2.7%. Among households with catastrophic spending, medical products accounted for the largest share of out-of-pocket payments (46.7%). 0.9% of households were impoverished or further impoverished by out-of-pocket payments (2022).

UHC Watch says the household budget survey in Norway cannot be compared with other countries.

Who goes without care?

In 2024, 1.6% of people had unmet need for health care: 4.3% in the poorest fifth and 0.5% in the richest. In 2024, 7.4% of people had unmet need for dental care: 14.5% in the poorest fifth and 1.3% in the richest. 4.3% of people needing care could not afford a prescribed medicine (2019).

How much do people pay out of pocket?

Out-of-pocket payments were 13.7% of current spending on health in 2023. The median across 53 countries is 22.0%. Households spent 2.8% of their budget on out-of-pocket payments for health care (2022).

How much does government spend on health?

Public spending on health was 8.1% of GDP in 2023. The median across 53 countries is 5.5%. Health took 17.4% of government spending (2023).

Where are the gaps in coverage?

Main gaps in population coverage

Because the basis for entitlement is permanent residence, most of the population is covered. However, people without a residence or working permit (including undocumented migrants and asylum seekers who are not registered with a reception centre) are entitled to publicly financed emergency care, childbirth and treatment of communicable diseases only. Administrative barriers may delay access to health care for asylum seekers due to the time it takes to process applications for asylum and residence.

Main gaps in service coverage

Dental care coverage is limited and only available to children < 28. Glasses and contact lenses are not typically covered for adults. Long waiting times are an issue for specialist care.

Main gaps caused by user charges

User charges are applied to all health care except inpatient care and generally apply to anyone > 16. The only low-income households exempt from some co-payments (for outpatient prescribed medicines, medical products and diagnostic tests) are pensioners who receive a minimum old-age or disability pension. Although there is an annual cap on co-payments but it does not cover balance billing or extra billing for non-covered clinical services.
