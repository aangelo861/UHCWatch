How many households face financial hardship?

In 2023, 9.5% of households had catastrophic health spending. In the poorest fifth of households it was 27.4%; in the richest fifth, 4.0%. Among households with catastrophic spending, medicines accounted for the largest share of out-of-pocket payments (44.9%). 4.3% of households were impoverished or further impoverished by out-of-pocket payments (2023).

How much do people pay out of pocket?

Public spending on health was 4.8% of GDP in 2023. The median across 53 countries is 5.5%. Health took 12.1% of government spending (2023).

Where are the gaps in coverage?

Main gaps in population coverage

In 2022 about 13.5% of the population entitled to coverage was not covered by the National Health Insurance Company (CNAM). Those most likely to lack CNAM coverage are working-age people, unemployed people, inactive or self-employed people, people working in agriculture and living in poorer households and in rural areas and migrants without temporary or permanent resident permits. All residents, regardless of coverage status, and Ukrainian refugees with temporary protection status, are entitled to emergency care, outpatient primary care visits, treatment of selected diseases and outpatient medicines and medical products for selected conditions, without user charges. Refugees, asylum seekers and undocumented migrants can only access emergency care.

Main gaps in service coverage

Coverage of outpatient prescribed medicines, diagnostic tests and medical products is limited. Coverage of non-emergency dental care is also limited. Waiting times are an issue, particularly for diagnostic services and for some types of outpatient specialist visit in public hospitals. Informal payments are widespread, mainly in inpatient settings.

Main gaps caused by user charges

User charges are applied to outpatient medicines and medical products, usually in the form of reference pricing. Other covered health care is free at the point of use with referral but people tend to pay the full price out of pocket for inpatient supplies (informal payments), for non-covered services and for covered outpatient care without a referral to avoid waiting lists.
