How many households face financial hardship?

In 2024, 3.8% of households had catastrophic health spending. In the poorest fifth of households it was 11.7%; in the richest fifth, 4.2%. Among households with catastrophic spending, dental care accounted for the largest share of out-of-pocket payments (75.6%). 1.6% of households were impoverished or further impoverished by out-of-pocket payments (2024).

How much do people pay out of pocket?

Out-of-pocket payments were 11.2% of current spending on health in 2023. The median across 53 countries is 22.0%. Households spent 2.9% of their budget on out-of-pocket payments for health care (2024).

Where are the gaps in coverage?

Main gaps in population coverage

The basis for entitlement to publicly financed health care is payment of mandatory health contributions. As a result around 2% of the population is not covered by the Andorran Social Security Office (Caixa Andorrana de Seguretat Social or CASS). However, most of these people have some form of coverage through their home-country health system (e.g. teachers from France and Spain) or VHI. Only 0.1% of the population has no form of coverage at all.

Adult undocumented migrants are entitled to emergency care only, which they have to pay for fully out of pocket. If they are accredited as having a low income, the out-of-pocket payments can be waived. The Minister of Social Affairs can provide full entitlement (the same as residents) to people granted health protection.

Main gaps in service coverage

Although the publicly financed benefits package is comprehensive, access can be limited by the widespread use of retrospective reimbursement (applied to most outpatient care for most people) and staff shortages and waiting times for some specialist care.

Main gaps caused by user charges

User charges are applied to all types of health care in the form of percentage co-payments. There is also reference pricing for outpatient prescribed medicines and balance billing for medical products. Although there are exemptions in place for people with low incomes and for some chronic conditions, there is no cap on co-payments. A large share of the population (around 73% in 2023) has VHI covering co-payments for publicly financed health care.

VHI role in filling gaps in coverage

VHI plays mainly a complementary (user charges) role, covering user charges for covered care. It plays a minor substitutive role providing coverage for ‘passive residents’ (foreign residents with certain economic conditions and no working permit e.g. foreign retired people) who are required to purchase VHI. In 2023 73% of the population had complementary VHI covering user charges (co-payments). In 2023 it accounted for 18.1% of current spending on health. Expanding VHI is likely to increase inequality in access to health care.
