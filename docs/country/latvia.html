How many households face financial hardship?

In 2016, 15.0% of households had catastrophic health spending. In the poorest fifth of households it was 29.6%; in the richest fifth, 3.5%. Among households with catastrophic spending, medicines accounted for the largest share of out-of-pocket payments (62.0%). 4.2% of households were impoverished or further impoverished by out-of-pocket payments (2016).

Who goes without care?

In 2025, 7.6% of people had unmet need for health care: 13.5% in the poorest fifth and 3.3% in the richest. In 2025, 9.0% of people had unmet need for dental care: 19.0% in the poorest fifth and 2.3% in the richest. 11.8% of people needing care could not afford a prescribed medicine (2019).

How much do people pay out of pocket?

Out-of-pocket payments were 35.1% of current spending on health in 2023. The median across 53 countries is 22.0%. Households spent 6.6% of their budget on out-of-pocket payments for health care (2016).

How much does government spend on health?

Public spending on health was 4.3% of GDP in 2023. The median across 53 countries is 5.5%. Health took 9.9% of government spending (2023).

Where are the gaps in coverage?

Main gaps in population coverage

Because entitlement to publicly financed health care is based on residence, population coverage is near universal; all permanent residents are automatically covered and refugees are entitled to the same benefits as residents. The main gap in population coverage is for asylum seekers, who are only entitled to a limited set of publicly financed health care (primary care visits and treatment, psychiatric care and emergency care) and undocumented migrants, who are only entitled to publicly financed emergency care.

Main gaps in service coverage

Coverage of dental care for adults and medical products (glasses and hearing aids) is limited. Other types of health care that are not well covered include physiotherapy and rehabilitation, occupational health care, psychotherapy and termination of pregnancy (without medical justification). Waiting times are an issue for imaging diagnostic tests, some types of outpatient care (physiotherapy and rehabilitation) and some types of surgery[

Main gaps caused by user charges

User charges are applied to all types of health care, including in the form of percentage co-payments for outpatient prescribed medicines and medical products (which are also subject to balance billing), but there are exemptions for people with low incomes, children and on the basis of health-related criteria. Although there is a cap on co-payments, the cap does not apply to co-payments for outpatient prescribed medicines or medical products and is not linked to income.

VHI role in filling gaps in coverage

VHI does not play a significant role in filling gaps in coverage. It plays a mixed complementary (covering some dental care, some diagnostic tests and some co-payments) and supplementary role (providing people with faster access to health care with long waiting times) and is likely mainly to cover people with higher incomes. It does not play any role in filling gaps in population coverage. In 2023 VHI covered around 24% of the population and in 2023 it accounted for 5.2% of current spending on health (WHO, 2026).
