How many households face financial hardship?

In 2015, 2.6% of households had catastrophic health spending. In the poorest fifth of households it was 7.9%; in the richest fifth, 1.5%. Among households with catastrophic spending, medical products accounted for the largest share of out-of-pocket payments (57.3%). 1.8% of households were impoverished or further impoverished by out-of-pocket payments (2015).

Who goes without care?

In 2024, 3.1% of people had unmet need for health care: 4.6% in the poorest fifth and 2.7% in the richest. In 2024, 7.5% of people had unmet need for dental care: 14.9% in the poorest fifth and 1.6% in the richest. 5.5% of people needing care could not afford a prescribed medicine (2019).

How much do people pay out of pocket?

Out-of-pocket payments were 13.9% of current spending on health in 2024. The median across 53 countries is 22.0%. Households spent 2.7% of their budget on out-of-pocket payments for health care (2015).

How much does government spend on health?

Public spending on health was 7.8% of GDP in 2024. The median across 53 countries is 5.5%. Health took 16.9% of government spending (2024).

Where are the gaps in coverage?

Main gaps in population coverage

Because the basis for entitlement is residence most of the population is covered. However, people without a residence permit (including undocumented migrants and refugees who are not registered in the National Register) are not entitled to the full range of publicly financed health care and can only access emergency care. Although they are required to pay the full price of any care they receive out of pocket, in practice the regional Government can exempt people with low incomes who are unable to pay. Asylum seekers and victims of human trafficking are entitled to a limited set of health services provided in asylum centres or crisis centres. Administrative barriers may delay access to health care for asylum seekers due to the lengthy residence application and processing time (an average wait of 240 days for asylum seekers and 35 days for Ukrainian refugees in 2023).

Main gaps in service coverage

Medical products, including hearing aids and glasses, are not covered. Coverage of physiotherapy, chiropractic care and outpatient visits to psychologists is limited. Dental care is only free for people < 20 and assisted reproductive health services are not covered for people > 41. Waiting times are an issue for some elective treatment and psychiatric services. People living in Denmark for more than three years who require language interpreter services when accessing health care are required to pay a fixed co-payment, which can be quite high for some services.

Main gaps caused by user charges

Fixed co-payments are applied to dental care for people > 21. Percentage co-payments apply to outpatient prescribed medicines. Although there are some exempt groups, these are not based on income and people generally require to submit an online application. There is no overall cap on co-payments. Although there is a cap on co-payments for outpatient prescribed medicines, it is not linked to income.

VHI role in filling gaps in coverage

VHI plays a supplementary role offering access to private providers and faster treatment and a complementary role covering services like medical products (glasses and contact lenses) and user charges for outpatient medicines and dental care. It accounted for 2.5% of current spending on health in 2022. Expanding VHI is likely to increase inequality in access to health care.
