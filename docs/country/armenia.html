Armenia page

How many households face financial hardship?

In 2021, 17.1% of households had catastrophic health spending. In the poorest fifth of households it was 42.9%; in the richest fifth, 10.3%. Among households with catastrophic spending, medicines accounted for the largest share of out-of-pocket payments (45.6%). 7.8% of households were impoverished or further impoverished by out-of-pocket payments (2021).

How much do people pay out of pocket?

Out-of-pocket payments were 80.5% of current spending on health in 2023. The median across 53 countries is 22.0%. Households spent 8.1% of their budget on out-of-pocket payments for health care (2021).

Public spending on health was 1.5% of GDP in 2023. The median across 53 countries is 5.5%. Health took 5.5% of government spending (2023).

Where are the gaps in coverage?

Main gaps in population coverage

Entitlement to publicly financed health care is based on residence, but only half of the population has access to the full range of publicly financed health care. All residents and refugees are entitled to outpatient care (including primary care and some specialist visits but excluding dental care) and some inpatient care. Only some residents and refugees (military staff, civil servants or other social package beneficiaries, people who have cancer or TB or who require intensive care) and asylum seekers are entitled to other publicly financed health care.

Main gaps in service coverage

Publicly financed coverage of inpatient care is limited for half of the population. Coverage of outpatient prescribed medicines, medical products, diagnostic tests and dental care is limited for the whole population. Covered people tend to face long waiting times for inpatient care and there are no policies to reduce waits. Informal payments are high, especially for inpatient care. There is a shortage of physicians outside Yerevan. Public spending on health is very low.

Main gaps caused by user charges

Co-payments are applied to publicly financed outpatient prescribed medicines, diagnostic tests, dental care treatment and inpatient care for some groups of people, often in the form of percentage co-payments. Exemptions from co-payments are complex and are not yet applied automatically. There is no overall cap on co-payments.
